# Historical and Heritage Styles

Period identities and their revivals. Use them as principle sources; a revival succeeds when it understands why the original worked. Index: `../style-catalogue.md`.

## Victorian

- **Also known as:** Victoriana; overlaps high Victorian eclecticism.
- **Period and origins:** Britain, 1837-1901 (reign of Victoria, documented); industrialised ornament, mass-printed ephemera, eclectic revivalism.
- **Philosophy:** abundance as virtue and status; ornament demonstrates care, wealth and moral seriousness; the machine made decoration democratic.
- **Visual characteristics:** dense ornament, engraved illustration, ornate borders and frames, layered pattern (wallpaper logic), dark rich interiors.
- **Geometry and composition:** symmetrical, framed, centred; hierarchy by increasing elaboration.
- **Colour:** deep jewel tones (burgundy, forest, navy), gold accents, sepia; later aniline brights (documented dye history).
- **Typography:** extreme variety on one page: fat faces, Tuscans, ornamented display, slab serifs; the playbill model.
- **Materials and finish:** carved wood, cast iron, velvet, etched glass, gilt.
- **Lighting and depth:** engraving-style shading, vignettes, candlelit warmth.
- **Motion and interaction:** rarely native; flourish-drawing animations suit it.
- **Use for:** heritage brands, gin/apothecary packaging, theatres, period storytelling, certificates.
- **Avoid when:** clarity, speed or modern trust is required; dense ornament suffocates UI.
- **Common mistakes:** random ornament without the framing logic; illegible display type at text sizes; sepia filter as the entire idea.
- **Accessibility:** ornamented type fails small; reserve for display, pair with a readable text face; check gold-on-dark contrast.
- **Implementation:** ornament assets are heavy; SVG borders scale better than raster; printing gold needs real foil or honest simulation.
- **Complements:** Gothic Revival, Steampunk, Luxury Editorial (carefully).
- **Conflicts:** Flat, Swiss, Minimalism, Y2K.
- **Transferable principles:** framing as hierarchy; typographic variety under a strong centring rule; craft density as a signal of care. Transfers to label design and certificate-grade documents.
- **Brief example:** "Small-batch bitters label: engraved botanical, three-tier playbill type, gold rule frame on bottle-green."
- **QA:** text sizes use a plain companion face; frame system consistent; ornament vectors not blurry raster; palette stays in the jewel register.

## Art Nouveau

- **Also known as:** Jugendstil (Germany), Secession (Vienna), Stile Liberty (Italy).
- **Period and origins:** Europe, c. 1890-1910 (documented); Horta, Guimard, Mucha, Tiffany; reaction against academic historicism, embracing natural form.
- **Philosophy:** art in everything; the organic line unifies structure and ornament; nature as the master pattern-book.
- **Visual characteristics:** whiplash curves, botanical and female figures, flowing hair and vines, integrated lettering, stained-glass colour cells.
- **Geometry and composition:** sinuous asymmetry; borders grown from the content; vertical formats.
- **Colour:** muted naturals with jewelled accents; mustard, olive, dusty rose, peacock.
- **Typography:** custom flowing letterforms merged with illustration; organic serifs and display faces.
- **Materials and finish:** wrought iron, stained glass, ceramic, lithographic flatness with outlines.
- **Lighting and depth:** flat cells with outline (cloisonné logic); gentle gradients inside shapes.
- **Motion and interaction:** line-growth animation is the natural move.
- **Use for:** beverage and cosmetics packaging, cultural posters, boutique hospitality, decorative SVG borders.
- **Avoid when:** engineering credibility or data clarity is the brief; curves fight tabular content.
- **Common mistakes:** clip-art swirls without the structural-line logic; Mucha pastiche heads on unrelated products.
- **Accessibility:** decorative type is display-only; outline-cell illustration survives contrast checks better than painterly styles.
- **Implementation:** genuinely hard to draw well; Bézier skill decides quality; SVG-native style when done right.
- **Complements:** Organic/Biomorphic, Bohemian, Luxury Editorial.
- **Conflicts:** Brutalism, Swiss, Pixel Art, Corporate Swiss.
- **Transferable principles:** ornament grown from structure rather than applied; line as continuous energy; frame and content as one drawing. Transfers to logo lockups and illustrated borders.
- **Brief example:** "Absinthe rebrand: whiplash vine frame integrating the wordmark, flat stained-glass palette, lithograph texture."
- **QA:** curves continuous (no kinked Béziers); lettering integrated not pasted; palette muted-plus-jewel; body copy in a plain face.

## Art Deco

- **Also known as:** Style Moderne; name from the 1925 Paris Exposition (documented).
- **Period and origins:** France then worldwide, c. 1920s-1930s; Chrysler Building, Cassandre's posters; machine-age luxury after WWI.
- **Philosophy:** glamour through geometry; the machine and the metropolis as objects of desire; luxury made graphic.
- **Visual characteristics:** sunbursts, chevrons, stepped forms (ziggurats), fan motifs, streamlined figures, gold on black.
- **Geometry and composition:** strict symmetry, vertical emphasis, radiating construction, stepped hierarchy.
- **Colour:** black, gold, cream, jade, lacquer red; metallics essential.
- **Typography:** geometric display faces, high waistlines, inline/bifurcated details (Broadway lineage); letterspaced capitals.
- **Materials and finish:** chrome, lacquer, exotic veneers, frosted glass, brass.
- **Lighting and depth:** polished reflections, rim light, theatrical spotlighting.
- **Motion and interaction:** radial reveals, metallic sheens sweeping; keep it stately.
- **Use for:** hotels, cocktail and spirits, cinema, gala events, premium fintech wanting heritage glamour.
- **Avoid when:** approachability or utility is the brief; Deco reads formal and exclusive.
- **Common mistakes:** Gatsby-party cliché (gold glitter everywhere), asymmetric layouts that break its symmetry logic, flimsy thin gold text failing contrast.
- **Accessibility:** gold-on-black often fails AA at text sizes, reserve for display; letterspaced caps need generous size.
- **Implementation:** pattern systems vectorise beautifully; metallic effects need gradient discipline (one light direction).
- **Complements:** Dark Luxury, Luxury Editorial, Cinematic.
- **Conflicts:** Grunge, Scandinavian, Neo-Brutalism, Clay.
- **Transferable principles:** radiating and stepped construction as hierarchy; symmetry as formality dial; metallic restraint (one metal, used sparsely). Transfers to award certificates, event identities, dark dashboards' accent logic.
- **Brief example:** "Rooftop-bar identity: fan-motif mark, letterspaced geometric caps, black/cream/single-gold, symmetric menus."
- **QA:** symmetry consistent; one metallic used sparingly; display/text face separation enforced; motifs from one construction family.

## Mid-Century Modern

- **Also known as:** MCM; overlaps Googie (commercial, interpretation) and Scandinavian modern.
- **Period and origins:** c. 1945-1969, USA and Europe (Eames, Saarinen, Girard, Rand's corporate work); post-war optimism, new materials (plywood, fibreglass).
- **Philosophy:** optimistic functionalism; good modern design for the booming middle class; playfulness within reason.
- **Visual characteristics:** organic-meets-geometric shapes (boomerangs, kidneys), tapered legs sensibility, atomic motifs, textured flat illustration.
- **Geometry and composition:** asymmetric balance, diagonal energy, confident white space with playful accents.
- **Colour:** mustard, teal, burnt orange, olive, warm wood on cream; saturated but earthy.
- **Typography:** humanist and geometric sans of the era; friendly scripts for accents; Rand-style wit.
- **Materials and finish:** teak, moulded plywood, fibreglass, boucle; print textures (overprint, misregistration charm).
- **Lighting and depth:** flat illustration with texture; soft product photography warmth.
- **Motion and interaction:** bouncy, cheerful easing; cutout-animation feel.
- **Use for:** coffee, furniture, travel, hospitality, brands selling optimism and craft heritage.
- **Avoid when:** cutting-edge tech credibility is required; reads nostalgic by definition.
- **Common mistakes:** generic "retro" soup mixing decades; texture filters hiding weak composition; palette drifting neon.
- **Accessibility:** era palettes often mid-contrast; verify text pairs; textured backgrounds need scrims.
- **Implementation:** texture (grain, overprint) needs tasteful raster or SVG filter budget.
- **Complements:** Scandinavian, Retro, Vector Art, Pop Art (adjacent).
- **Conflicts:** Cyberpunk, Dark Luxury, Brutalism (graphic), Y2K.
- **Transferable principles:** optimism as a tunable quality; organic/geometric tension; texture as warmth layer on flat systems. Transfers to illustration systems and brand voice.
- **Brief example:** "Roastery brand: kidney-shaped mark, mustard/teal/cream, grain-textured flat illustrations of brewing gear."
- **QA:** decade references consistent (not generic retro); texture supports not hides shapes; contrast verified; wit present but controlled.

## Memphis Design

- **Also known as:** Memphis Group, Memphis Milano.
- **Period and origins:** Milan, founded 1981 around Ettore Sottsass (documented); disbanded late 1980s; revived heavily in 2010s digital illustration.
- **Philosophy:** against good taste: colour, pattern and joke-geometry liberate design from functionalist sobriety; furniture as communication.
- **Visual characteristics:** squiggles (bacterio print), terrazzo, clashing primaries and pastels, black-and-white stripes, impossible object furniture.
- **Geometry and composition:** deliberate imbalance, stacked unrelated shapes, pattern collision.
- **Colour:** pastels against saturated primaries, plus black/white graphic patterns.
- **Typography:** playful geometric display, outlined and shadowed letters.
- **Materials and finish:** plastic laminate celebrated (anti-precious), printed patterns.
- **Lighting and depth:** flat with fake isometric shadows; cartoon depth.
- **Motion and interaction:** springy, toy-like, pattern-cycling.
- **Use for:** youth brands, creative tools, event identities, social content needing loud joy.
- **Avoid when:** trust, calm or data clarity is needed; it is deliberately anti-serious.
- **Common mistakes:** using it as "fun confetti" garnish on a sober product; forgetting the anti-design intellectual core; unreadable text on pattern.
- **Accessibility:** pattern backgrounds demand solid text panels; motion-heavy pattern can trigger discomfort, provide reduced-motion.
- **Implementation:** pattern libraries as SVG; strict z-order discipline or it becomes mud.
- **Complements:** Maximalism, Pop Art, Y2K.
- **Conflicts:** Minimalism, Luxury Editorial, Aerospace Instrumentation, Corporate Swiss.
- **Transferable principles:** rule-breaking as a system (its clashes are composed); pattern as voice; humour as differentiator. Transfers to brand pattern libraries and empty states.
- **Brief example:** "Design-tool launch campaign: terrazzo field, three stacked impossible shapes, outlined display type on solid panel."
- **QA:** clashes composed not random; text always on solid; patterns from a defined set; humour fits brand register.

## Retro

- **Also known as:** vintage revival; umbrella term, always era-specific in practice.
- **Period and origins:** revivalism is perennial; "retro" as a design strategy mainstream from c. 1970s onward (interpretation); always references a specific earlier decade.
- **Philosophy:** borrowed memory: evoke an era's codes to transfer its emotional associations (trust, fun, quality) to a modern product.
- **Visual characteristics:** era-dependent: 50s diner chrome and script, 70s earth stripes and rounded slabs, 80s airbrush and chrome.
- **Geometry and composition:** follows the referenced decade; badges and lockups common.
- **Colour:** era palettes (70s: avocado/harvest gold; 50s: pastel + chrome; 80s: sunset gradients).
- **Typography:** period faces or faithful revivals; script badges, rounded slabs, chrome bevels per era.
- **Materials and finish:** print artefacts (halftone, misregistration, paper yellowing) as authenticity signals.
- **Lighting and depth:** per era; halftone shading, airbrush glow.
- **Motion and interaction:** era tech metaphors (CRT flicker, film grain) used sparingly.
- **Use for:** food/beverage, apparel, music, campaigns needing instant warmth or nostalgia.
- **Avoid when:** the audience lacks the memory (nostalgia misfires), or innovation is the message.
- **Common mistakes:** decade soup (mixing 50s script with 80s chrome), filter-only retro with modern layout underneath, illegible authentic-but-tiny type.
- **Accessibility:** aged-paper backgrounds reduce contrast; verify; grain kept off text.
- **Implementation:** commit to one decade's full system (type, palette, texture, layout logic) or it reads fake.
- **Complements:** Mid-Century Modern, Y2K, Retro-Futurism, Pop Art (era-matched).
- **Conflicts:** Futuristic, Corporate Swiss, Spatial/Immersive.
- **Transferable principles:** era codes are systems, research them as systems; nostalgia is audience-relative; artefacts of production (halftone, grain) carry authenticity. Transfers to any heritage storytelling.
- **Brief example:** "Hot-sauce line: 1970s palette, rounded slab wordmark, halftone chilli illustration, kraft label."
- **QA:** one decade only, verified against period references; texture authentic to that era's print tech; modern legibility preserved; nostalgia target audience confirmed.

## Y2K

- **Also known as:** millennium aesthetic; overlaps "Frutiger Aero" successor discourse (interpretation, online-community terminology).
- **Period and origins:** c. 1997-2004; consumer-tech optimism (iMac translucency 1998, documented), early web, rave flyers, metallic futures.
- **Philosophy:** the future is friendly, glossy and digital; technology as toy and jewellery.
- **Visual characteristics:** chrome blobs, lens flares, translucent plastic, bubble type, pixel fonts beside airbrush gradients, early-3D renders.
- **Geometry and composition:** floating elements, orbit layouts, aggressive diagonals.
- **Colour:** silver, baby blue, hot pink, lime, iridescent sweeps on white or black.
- **Typography:** bubble and blobby display, techno fonts (Eurostile-lineage), pixel accents.
- **Materials and finish:** gloss everything: chrome, jelly, frosted translucent plastic.
- **Lighting and depth:** specular highlights, flares, plastic subsurface glow.
- **Motion and interaction:** shimmer sweeps, bouncy 3D rotations.
- **Use for:** music, fashion drops, Gen-Z social campaigns, products playing with techno-nostalgia.
- **Avoid when:** seriousness or timelessness is required; strongly dated by design.
- **Common mistakes:** muddying chrome with too many hues; flares over faces/text; mistaking generic vaporwave for Y2K (different palettes and dates).
- **Accessibility:** gloss and gradient backgrounds wreck contrast; solid panels for text; flare motion needs reduced-motion variant.
- **Implementation:** chrome type is real 3D or careful gradient maps; heavy raster, plan asset weight.
- **Complements:** Holographic, Retro-Futurism, Maximalism.
- **Conflicts:** Japanese Minimalism, Luxury Editorial, Sustainable/Circular.
- **Transferable principles:** material optimism (gloss = friendliness) as a dial; period-accurate tech artefacts as voice. Transfers to campaign skins over stable product UI.
- **Brief example:** "Album microsite: chrome blob wordmark, baby-blue sky render, pixel-font tracklist on frosted panel."
- **QA:** palette within the Y2K register; text on solid/frosted panels passes AA; chrome highlights share one environment logic; dated-on-purpose confirmed with user.

## Retro-Futurism

- **Also known as:** yesterday's tomorrow; includes atomic-age futurism, space-age, cassette futurism (sub-flavours, community terms).
- **Period and origins:** source material c. 1930s-1970s visions of the future (World's Fairs, pulp sci-fi, NASA-era graphics, documented); named as a retrospective genre later.
- **Philosophy:** depict the future as a past era imagined it; the gap between that optimism and reality is the emotional engine (hopeful, ironic or melancholy).
- **Visual characteristics:** ray-gun curves, fins, bubble domes, analogue dials and CRT readouts, space-age typography, propaganda-poster confidence.
- **Geometry and composition:** streamlined teardrops, radial boosts, heroic low angles.
- **Colour:** era-dependent: atomic cream/red/teal, or 70s NASA white/red/blue worm-era palettes.
- **Typography:** extended techno faces (Eurostile/Microgramma lineage), stencil, chrome scripts per source era.
- **Materials and finish:** brushed steel, bakelite, blinking indicator lamps, perforated grilles.
- **Lighting and depth:** dramatic rim light, airbrush gradients, glow of phosphor screens.
- **Motion and interaction:** mechanical switches, CRT scanline reveals, countdown language.
- **Use for:** games, entertainment, space/tech brands with wit, museums, editorial about technology.
- **Avoid when:** real engineering credibility is the point (it signals fiction), or the irony would undercut the message.
- **Common mistakes:** mixing source decades incoherently; adding modern flat UI inside the fiction; prop detail overwhelming information.
- **Accessibility:** phosphor-glow text fails contrast fast; dial-heavy layouts need text equivalents.
- **Implementation:** commit to one imagined-era's tech level consistently (interface fiction discipline).
- **Complements:** Y2K, Steampunk (sibling logic), Cinematic, Aerospace Instrumentation (as costume source, carefully).
- **Conflicts:** Organic Minimalism, Scandinavian, Corporate Swiss.
- **Transferable principles:** coherent fictional tech level as a worldbuilding rule; optimism/irony as a tone dial; diegetic UI thinking. Transfers to product storytelling and game/launch campaigns.
- **Brief example:** "Space-coffee campaign: atomic-age poster, finned rocket percolator, cream/red/teal, confident slogan caps."
- **QA:** one source era throughout; fiction level consistent (no anachronisms); information readable past the props; tone (hope vs irony) matches brief.

## Steampunk

- **Also known as:** Victorian science fiction aesthetic; term coined c. 1987 in literary context (documented).
- **Period and origins:** literary genre 1980s (Jeter, Gibson/Sterling's The Difference Engine 1990); visual subculture growth 1990s-2000s; imagines Victorian-era technology extrapolated.
- **Philosophy:** romance of visible mechanism: brass, steam and craft against the sealed black box; technology you could repair with a spanner.
- **Visual characteristics:** gears, rivets, pressure gauges, brass and copper, leather, etched ornament on machinery, goggles-and-airship iconography.
- **Geometry and composition:** mechanical symmetry with ornamental framing; exploded-mechanism fascination.
- **Colour:** brass, copper, oxblood, oak, verdigris, soot black, parchment.
- **Typography:** Victorian display (Tuscans, ornamented slabs) plus engraved plates and stamped metal labels.
- **Materials and finish:** brushed brass, riveted iron, worn leather, etched glass.
- **Lighting and depth:** warm lamplight, steam haze, metallic speculars.
- **Motion and interaction:** gear trains, needle gauges, pressure hisses; mechanisms should visibly cause effects.
- **Use for:** games, themed hospitality, escape rooms, book covers, craft spirits.
- **Avoid when:** modern efficiency or clean trust is the message; dense props crush usability in real UI.
- **Common mistakes:** gears glued on with no mechanical logic (the cardinal sin); gear trains that could not turn; mixing digital-era UI into the fiction.
- **Accessibility:** parchment/brass palettes are low contrast; gauge metaphors need labelled values.
- **Implementation:** heavy illustrative assets; SVG gear systems must share module/pitch to look meshable.
- **Complements:** Victorian, Gothic Revival, Retro-Futurism.
- **Conflicts:** Flat, Swiss, Y2K, Organic Minimalism.
- **Transferable principles:** visible causality (controls visibly drive outcomes) transfers to honest UI feedback; mechanism as ornament only when it could work. Transfers to loading states and process visualisation.
- **Brief example:** "Escape-room brand: brass gauge logo with plausible needle sweep, riveted frame system, parchment menus with stamped labels."
- **QA:** every gear train could mesh; one metal family dominates; labels legible on texture; fiction consistent (no anachronistic UI).

## Gothic Revival

- **Also known as:** Neo-Gothic, Victorian Gothic (architectural phase).
- **Period and origins:** Britain, c. 1740s onward, peaking 19th century (Pugin's Contrasts 1836, Ruskin's writings, Palace of Westminster, documented); revival of medieval Gothic forms.
- **Philosophy:** verticality and ornament as moral and spiritual seriousness; truth to structure (Pugin: ornament the construction, never construct ornament, documented principle).
- **Visual characteristics:** pointed arches, tracery, ribbed vaults, finials, blackletter, stained glass, trefoil/quatrefoil motifs.
- **Geometry and composition:** vertical aspiration, pointed-arch module, symmetry with hierarchical elaboration toward the sacred/center.
- **Colour:** stone greys, oak, deep reds and blues of stained glass, gold leaf, black.
- **Typography:** blackletter/textura for display (illegible at length, use sparingly); incised Roman capitals as companion.
- **Materials and finish:** carved stone, dark oak, leaded glass, wrought iron.
- **Lighting and depth:** dramatic directional light through tracery; deep shadow as atmosphere.
- **Motion and interaction:** slow reveals, candle-flicker restraint; solemn pacing.
- **Use for:** universities, churches, whisky/beer heritage, fantasy IP, metal/music culture, ceremonial documents.
- **Avoid when:** friendliness or modern efficiency is the brief; blackletter carries heavy cultural baggage, deploy knowingly.
- **Common mistakes:** blackletter body copy; horror-movie clichés when heritage dignity was wanted; ornament without the structural-truth logic.
- **Accessibility:** blackletter is display-only; stained-glass palettes need contrast checks; heavy dark layouts need clear landmarks.
- **Transferable principles:** Pugin's rule (decorate structure, never fake it) applies to all UI chrome; vertical hierarchy as aspiration; module-based tracery logic for pattern systems.
- **Implementation:** tracery vectorises well from real geometric construction (compass logic), not freehand.
- **Complements:** Victorian, Steampunk, Dark Luxury.
- **Conflicts:** Flat, Y2K, Clay, Scandinavian.
- **Brief example:** "University society crest and certificate: pointed-arch frame, quatrefoil motif, incised caps with blackletter title line, stone/oxblood/gold."
- **QA:** blackletter display-only; ornament follows structure; motifs geometrically constructed; tone dignified not costume-horror.
