# Workflow: Design Research

Investigate design history, movements, practitioners, materials and technical approaches without fabricating authority.

**Inputs:** the research question and what decision it will inform.
**Outputs:** findings with sources and dates, separated into established fact, informed interpretation, and open uncertainty.
**Load:** [../references/design-history.md](../references/design-history.md), [../references/style-catalogue.md](../references/style-catalogue.md); web research tools when available.

## Steps

1. **Anchor to the decision.** Research serves a design choice. Write the question as "we need to know X to decide Y"; scope ends when Y can be decided.

2. **Start from the skill's own references.** design-history.md and the style catalogue may already answer the question; they also flag which claims were verified and which are general knowledge.

3. **Go to strong sources when tools allow.** Prefer, in order: primary sources and archives (museum collections such as MoMA, V&A, Cooper Hewitt; foundation archives; patent filings; original manuals), institutional scholarship, credible specialist publications. Treat listicles and mood-board sites as leads, never as evidence.

4. **Verify before repeating.** Dates, attributions, nationalities, "firsts" and quotations are the most commonly corrupted facts in design writing. Cross-check any claim that will be stated as fact. "Firsts" almost always need softening to "among the earliest".

5. **Record dates on perishable claims.** Platform guidelines, standards versions, tool capabilities and material availability change; write "as of [date]" on each such claim.

6. **Separate the three registers in the output:**
   - **Documented fact:** sourced, checkable.
   - **Interpretation:** reasoned reading of the evidence, labelled as such.
   - **Unknown:** what could not be verified in this environment. Say so; never backfill with plausible invention.

7. **Extract the transferable principle.** End each researched movement or example with the principle it offers today's problem, per [cross-disciplinary-transfer.md](cross-disciplinary-transfer.md). History without extraction is trivia.

8. **Cite.** Name sources specifically enough that the user can check them. No invented books, designers, dates or museum holdings, ever. An honest "unverified" outranks a confident fabrication.

## Gate

Done only when: the anchoring decision can now be made; every factual claim is sourced or labelled unverified; perishable claims carry dates; at least one transferable principle is stated. Research that returns only atmosphere ("the Bauhaus valued simplicity") has failed.
